Pooled: Exploring Distributed AI Inference

Testing whether everyday devices can contribute browser-based compute to a peer-to-peer AI inference setup.

← Back to Jason Harvey’s portfolio

Experiment in progress

What we set out to explore

This experiment explores Pooled, an open-source project that investigates peer-to-peer, browser-based distributed AI inference. The idea is to connect devices so that available resources may be pooled rather than relying on one machine alone.

The aim was to test a Windows desktop and an iPhone as potential peers, understand the connection requirements, and distinguish what the interface reports from what has actually been proven.

Credit: Pooled is the work of Nehanth Narendrula. This page documents our hands-on testing and observations; it does not claim authorship of the Pooled project.

Test setup

Toucan · Windows PC

Ryzen 5 3400G desktop with AMD Radeon Vega 11 graphics. Used as the first browser peer.

Sparrow · iPhone

Used as a second device to investigate joining the same peer session from a mobile browser.

Browser connection

Tested the local development page and the hosted Pooled website, with browser-based peer connectivity.

What we observed

  • Localhost is device-specific. Opening localhost on the iPhone points to the iPhone itself, not the Windows PC hosting the local page.
  • LAN addressing helped with access. We switched to the PC’s local network address (192.168.0.155) so the phone could attempt to reach the PC-hosted page while on the same network.
  • Peer connectivity was not straightforward. Browser, network and WebRTC connection behaviour caused difficulties; relay/TURN connectivity was also investigated.
  • The interface displayed two devices and 4.5 GB of combined allocated capacity (4 GB on the PC and 0.5 GB on the iPhone) during the test.
Important: The displayed combined capacity is an interface-reported allocation, not proof that the iPhone successfully processed model layers or that inference was completed across both devices.

Current conclusion

We made useful progress understanding the setup and got far enough to see both devices represented in the session. However, we have not yet verified end-to-end distributed inference across the PC and iPhone.

To call the experiment successful, the next test needs to demonstrate a real inference request, confirm that work is distributed between the peers, and capture evidence such as device-level activity, logs or a reproducible output. Until then, this remains a connectivity and capacity-allocation experiment.

Next steps

  • Re-test peer connectivity on a stable network and document the exact browser/device combination.
  • Check whether both peers become active for an actual inference request.
  • Capture logs or other evidence showing which device processes which part of the workload.
  • Record performance, reliability and any mobile-browser limitations before drawing conclusions.
